# Public-Copy

PC-1 Identify audience, genre and established facts. Organize value introductions around outcomes, mechanisms and practical benefits so readers can understand the current solution independently.

PC-2 Explain value through concrete capabilities and mechanisms. Express rationale as goals and benefits. Quantified benefits need evidence; label unverified benefits as expected.

PC-3 Value introductions contain outcomes and benefits. Place problem costs, historical comparisons, negative consequences and risk analysis in sections explicitly requested or required by the genre. State conditions for accurate use as clear applicability. Preserve material facts without disguising them.

PC-4 Use what was delivered, how it works and what value it provides as review questions, adapting structure to the genre. Keep reader-relevant mechanisms; remove internal drafting history, author self-certification and discarded-option explanations.

PC-5 Generate titles, filenames, summaries, captions and metadata from current results. Commits and PRs describe current behavior, including triggering conditions, compatibility effects and verification when needed. Preserve facts required by quotations, audits and migration notes.

PC-6 Review prose and packaging for facts, audience needs, incremental information and consistency with the observed state. Identify the fact, mechanism, condition or necessary implication contributed by each sentence; consolidate explanations that merely change wording. Check whole-paragraph information density in English as well, avoiding successive summary sentences used to fill space. Requested length should be supported by evidence-grounded content. End when the content is complete. Retain a closing sentence only when it adds a concrete consequence, decision or action; otherwise omit it. Explain practical value through a specific consequence of the mechanism, keeping illustrative numbers distinct from measured benefits.

PC-7 Actors, actions, inputs, sequence and responsibility in migration, installation and operating steps must follow established material. Where responsibility is unspecified, describe verifiable product behavior or acceptance conditions; ask only when execution depends on the missing detail and continue settled work. If the source only says the product groups requests, verify the grouping outcome while leaving the actor unspecified; if it assigns grouping to the caller, preserve that operation. Necessary logical implications can explain value; additional architectural assumptions need a source.
